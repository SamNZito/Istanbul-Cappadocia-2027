Day By Day

Thu 20 May · Day 0Depart
Fly

Evening departure JFK or EWR on Turkish Airlines. Ten hours nonstop. You sleep on the plane and land mid-afternoon.

Fri 21 May · Day 1Land soft, eat well
Day

Land IST around 3–4pm. Private transfer to Beyoğlu, ~50 minutes. Check in, ninety-minute nap maximum, then out — you're only seven hours off and fighting it now pays off all week.

Night

Dinner at Karaköy Lokantası — classic Turkish, mid-priced, never wrong. Then walk up through Galata for cocktails at Alexandra Cocktail Bar, or just stay in and use the Soho House rooftop.

Deliberately easy. Saturday is the night.

Sat 22 May · Day 2Old City, then the big one
Day

Hagia Sophia at opening — book the timed entry online, the walk-up line is punishing. Then the Blue Mosque and the Basilica Cistern, 336 Roman columns underground, two of them standing on carved Medusa heads.

Lunch, then Süleymaniye Mosque — better architecture than the Blue Mosque, better view over the Golden Horn, a fraction of the crowd. Finish in the Grand Bazaar, 4,000 shops running since 1461.

Night

Dinner at Mikla or Neolokal — Michelin-recognized, $70–95 a head, a fraction of the New York equivalent. Book weeks out.

Then Ortaköy waterfront — Sortie or Reina. Saturday is peak and the rooms are full. Dress code is enforced on men: collared shirt, real shoes. No shorts, no athletic sneakers, they will turn you away.

Sun 23 May · Day 3Bosphorus and the Asian side
Day

Private boat on the Bosphorus, 2–3 hours, $200–280 for the four of you. Ottoman palaces, waterfront mansions, both continents. This is where the exchange rate starts feeling absurd.

Lunch on the water in Bebek. Then the ferry to Kadıköy on the Asian side — a dollar, twenty minutes, tea service on deck.

Night

Çiya Sofrası in Kadıköy for regional Anatolian food you won't find on the European side. Then the bar streets around Kadife Sokak — this is where Istanbul actually drinks. Cheaper, younger, no dress code, no tourists.

Ferry back across at 1am with the city lit up. Best twenty minutes of the trip.

Mon 24 May · Day 4Hammam, then fly to Cappadocia
Day

Kılıç Ali Paşa Hamamı in Karaköy — built in 1580. Steam room, then an attendant takes a layer off you with a coarse kese mitt, then a foam massage on hot marble. $60–90 and non-negotiable.

Afternoon flight IST → Kayseri, 90 minutes. Transfer to Ürgüp, about an hour.

Night

Dinner at the cave hotel, drinks on the terrace, in bed early. The balloon pickup is 4:30am. This is the one night you don't push it.

Tue 25 May · Day 5The balloon
Dawn

Sunrise hot air balloon. Hundreds launching at once over eroded volcanic spires. Book direct with Butterfly, Royal, or Voyager — $180–250 each. Balloons fly roughly 90% of May mornings, among the highest rates of the year. Back in bed by 9am.

Day

Göreme Open-Air Museum — Byzantine cave churches with frescoes still on the walls. Then Derinkuyu underground city, eight levels carved into rock, sheltered 20,000 people, with stables, chapels and ventilation shafts.

Golden hour: ATVs through Love Valley and Rose Valley, ~$45 each. May means wildflowers in the valleys.

Night

Dinner in a cave restaurant in Ürgüp, then terrace drinks over the valley. Quiet by design — Cappadocia isn't a nightlife town and that's fine, it's the one night you'll want it that way.

Wed 26 May · Day 6Backup morning, back to the city
Day

This morning is your weather backup for the balloon if Tuesday got cancelled. If it flew, use it for Uçhisar Castle and a walk down through Pigeon Valley instead.

Afternoon flight back to Istanbul.

Night

Rooftops. 360 Istanbul or 16 Roof at the Swissôtel — skyline, Bosphorus, DJ sets. Wednesday is a real night out in Istanbul, not a dead one.

Thu 27 May · Day 7The neighborhoods, then the last night
Day

Balat and Fener — painted Ottoman houses on cobblestone hills, the old Jewish and Greek quarters. No tour buses. Then the Spice Bazaar for anything you're actually taking home, and Galata Tower for the last view.

Night

Zübeyir Ocakbaşı — you sit around an open coal pit and they grill in front of you. Roughly $20 a head and better than most steakhouses.

Then a proper Karaköy crawl to close it out. Last night, no schedule, no alarm the next morning until the airport run.

Fri 28 May · Day 8Home
Fly

Late morning departure, land JFK the same calendar day — you gain the seven hours back flying west. Everyone's at work Monday having slept a night in their own bed.

Things That Will Bite You

  • The Taksim bar scam. A friendly English-speaking local walks your group to a bar; the bill arrives for thousands and the door is blocked. It runs specifically on groups of foreign men. Never follow a stranger to a venue. Pick your own bars.
  • Club dress codes are real. Ortaköy will turn away four guys in shorts and sneakers without a second thought.
  • Alcohol is heavily taxed. $6–9 a beer, $10–14 a cocktail. Duty-free on arrival at IST is genuinely worth using for hotel drinks.
  • Decline dynamic currency conversion at every ATM and card terminal. It's a 5–10% skim. Always pay in lira.
  • Use BiTaksi or Uber, never a hailed street taxi. Uber in Istanbul dispatches licensed yellow cabs.
  • Mosque etiquette: long pants, shoes off, avoid Friday midday prayers.
