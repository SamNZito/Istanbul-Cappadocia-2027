1 · When To Go

Recommendation: Thursday 20 May – Friday 28 May 2027. Three reasons those exact dates:

  • It clears Kurban Bayramı. Eid al-Adha runs 15–19 May 2027. During it the Grand Bazaar and Spice Bazaar close entirely, banks shut, ATMs run dry, and domestic travel is chaos. It ends the evening of the 19th and everything reopens on the 20th.
  • Saturday 22 May lands mid-trip — that's your big night, when Istanbul's clubs actually run at capacity.
  • Cappadocia balloons fly roughly 90% of May mornings, among the highest fly rates of the year.

Both are excellent and it's close. Locals tend to rate September marginally higher for crowds; May wins on weather consistency and the balloon fly rate. If May 2027 doesn't work for everyone, mid-to-late September 2027 is the alternate — same plan, same budget.

2 · Entry & Passports

You need no visa. You do need to check your passports today.

  • US citizens are visa-free in Türkiye for 90 days within any 180-day period, for tourism. No e-visa, no application, no fee. This changed in 2024 — older guides still say you need an e-visa. You don't.
  • Passport must be valid at least six months beyond entry. For a 20 May 2027 arrival that means valid through at least 20 November 2027. Check all four passports this week — renewals can take months.
  • At least one blank page for the entry stamp.
  • No vaccination requirements for US travelers.
  • Register with STEP (the State Department's traveler enrollment program) before you fly. Free, two minutes, and it means the embassy can reach you.
  • Travel insurance is not required for entry but get it anyway — one policy each, roughly $50–90 for the trip.

3 · Laws You Must Know

Most of Türkiye feels like anywhere in Europe. But there are four areas where the law is genuinely stricter than you'd expect, and two of them have put tourists in prison. Read this section properly.

Do not insult the President, Atatürk, the flag, or the Turkish state

This is not a formality — it is actively prosecuted, including for social media posts made by foreigners.

  • Article 299 — insulting the President of the Republic is a criminal offense.
  • Law 5816 — insulting the memory of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, or defacing statues and images of him, carries up to five years in prison. The same applies to the Turkish flag.
  • Article 301 — publicly denigrating the Turkish nation or state institutions.

Practical rule for the group: no political jokes, no political posts while you're in the country, and treat Atatürk the way you'd treat a war memorial. His portrait is everywhere and he is genuinely revered. This costs you nothing to respect.

Do not take home anything old — including a rock

Exporting antiquities or historical artifacts is a serious crime carrying 5 to 12 years in prison plus heavy fines. Tourists have been arrested at the airport for picking up an interesting stone or a shard of broken pottery at an ancient site.

Rules: Take nothing off the ground at any ruin or beach. If you buy carpets, ceramics, coins or anything that might read as antique, buy only from authorized dealers and get a museum certificate plus the receipt for each item. Keep both in your carry-on. Without those papers at departure you can be detained.

Alcohol — the rule that sounds scary but isn't

Retail sale of alcohol is banned from 10pm to 6am. Shops, supermarkets and Tekel stores cannot sell after 10pm. Spirits above 40% ABV are off retail shelves entirely. Legal drinking age is 18.

This does not affect your nights out. Licensed bars, restaurants and clubs keep serving well past midnight — bars close around 1–2am, clubs run to 4–5am. The ban is on shops, not venues.

Two practical notes: buy anything you want in the hotel room before 10pm, and use the duty-free on arrival at IST — Turkish alcohol tax is steep, so a bottle bought at the airport saves real money. Public street drinking is restricted and socially frowned on. Drink in venues, not on the street.

Drugs — zero tolerance, no exceptions

Penalties are severe and apply to possession of any quantity, including cannabis. There is no tourist leniency and no version of this that ends well. Don't carry anything, don't accept anything, and be careful about who's in your group at a club.

The rest of the list

DronesRegister with the Turkish government before flying anything over 500g. Max altitude 120m. Commercial use needs separate approval. Easiest answer for a nine-day trip: leave it home.
PhotographyNever photograph military installations, police, or government buildings. Signs are posted; take them seriously.
MosquesFree to enter. Long pants, shoulders covered, shoes off. Avoid Friday midday prayers. Women need a head covering — usually provided at the door.
SmokingBanned indoors in all public places, including bars and restaurants. Enforced.
LGBTQ+Homosexuality is legal, but there are no anti-discrimination protections and Pride marches in Istanbul have been banned since 2015. Attitudes in Beyoğlu and Kadıköy are liberal; be more discreet elsewhere.
IDCarry a photo of your passport on your phone. Police can ask for identification.
TaxisRefusing a fare or not running the meter is illegal — but easier to avoid entirely by using BiTaksi or Uber.

Get all four to commit to 20–28 May 2027 in writing. Nothing is booked, nothing is paid. This is the step that historically kills group trips, and it's free.

Six workdays, requested seven months out — nobody's manager says no to that. Set Google Flights alerts on JFK/EWR → IST for the date range so you learn what a good fare looks like instead of guessing in January.

Skip the Istanbul Museum Pass. It costs €105 for 13 sites over five days and it covers Topkapı and the Harem — but it does not cover Hagia Sophia's main floor, the Basilica Cistern, or Dolmabahçe. Türkiye's only two-star. Two tasting menus, intimate room, choreographed service. The most serious Turkish fine dining anywhere in the world. Budget $150–200 a head — published lira prices move fast with inflation, so confirm at booking. Reserve in November 2026 for a May trip.

The one rule that matters most. Never follow a stranger to a bar. The Taksim scam runs specifically on groups of foreign men — a friendly English speaker offers to show you a great place, drinks appear, women join the table, and the bill lands at several thousand dollars with the door blocked. Pick your own venues from this list. If a room feels wrong, leave before you order. Emergency number in Türkiye: 112.

9 · Cappadocia

The balloon

Sunrise launch, hundreds of balloons over eroded volcanic spires. $180–250 each, booked direct with Butterfly Balloons, Royal Balloon or Voyager — skip resellers. Pickup 4:30am. Roughly 90% of May mornings fly. Schedule it your first morning so the second stays a backup.

Everything else

Göreme Open-Air MuseumUNESCO

Rock-cut Byzantine churches with original frescoes, painted by early Christian communities living in the stone.

Derinkuyu underground cityOr Kaymaklı

Eight levels carved into volcanic rock — stables, chapels, wineries, ventilation shafts. Sheltered 20,000 people from invading armies.

ATVs at golden hour~$45

Love Valley and Rose Valley on quads as the light drops. Wildflowers everywhere in May.

Uçhisar Castle & Pigeon ValleyHalf day

Rock fortress with the widest view in the region, then walk down through the valley to Göreme.

Avanos potteryOn the Kızılırmak

Red clay thrown on foot-powered wheels since the Hittites. Make one badly and take it home.

Rose Valley hikeFree

Also the best ground-level vantage point for watching the balloons if yours gets weathered out.

10 · Getting Around

  • From the airport: the M11 metro opened in 2024 — ride to Kağıthane, transfer to M2 for Taksim. 50–60 min, about ₺35. The Havaist bus is ₺180 and sits in traffic. On arrival day with bags, a private van transfer (~$40 for four) is worth it.
  • Istanbulkart: buy one each on day one from any Biletmatik machine. ₺165 for the card, load balance separately, ₺42 a ride with discounted transfers inside two hours. Covers metro, tram, bus, ferry, funicular and Marmaray.
  • The T1 tram runs directly past Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, the Grand Bazaar and the Spice Bazaar. You'll use it daily.
  • Funiculars beat hills. The Tünel — the second-oldest underground railway in the world, running since 1875 — hauls you from near Galata Bridge to the bottom of İstiklal. Kabataş–Taksim connects the tram to Taksim Square.
  • Ferries, ~₺49. Twenty minutes between continents with tea on deck. Do it once in daylight and once at night.
  • Taxis: BiTaksi or Uber only. Uber in Istanbul dispatches licensed yellow cabs. Never hail off the street.
  • Traffic warning: Istanbul has among the worst road congestion of any major city — drivers lose 53% extra time. A 30-minute drive becomes an hour in evening rush. Stay on rails and boats.
  • Cappadocia: fly IST → Kayseri (ASR) or Nevşehir (NAV), 90 min, $40–70. Hotels arrange transfers. No real public transport on the ground — book activities with pickup or hire a driver.

Money on the ground

  • Lira at roughly ₺47.7 to the dollar.
  • Withdraw from bank ATMs — Ziraat, İş Bankası, Garanti. Avoid standalone machines in tourist areas.
  • Always decline "pay in USD" at ATMs and card terminals. Dynamic currency conversion is a 5–10% skim.
  • Cards work almost everywhere in Istanbul. Carry cash in Cappadocia.
  • Tipping: 5–10% at restaurants, round up taxis, $5–10 for hammam attendants.
  • eSIM: Airalo or Holafly, $10–20 for the trip. Don't buy a local physical SIM — Turkey registers device IMEIs.
