The Pitch, In One Paragraph

Istanbul is the only city on earth that has been the capital of three separate empires and sits on two continents at once. You can eat a Michelin dinner for a third of the New York price, get scrubbed raw in a bathhouse built in 1580, take a private boat past Ottoman palaces for sixty-five dollars a head, and close the night in a club on the water. Then you fly ninety minutes east and float over a valley of volcanic spires at sunrise in a hot air balloon. Nine days, six workdays, ten hours in the air, and one seven-hour time shift you'll shake off in a morning.

Getting Around

From the airport — take the metro, not the bus

The M11 metro line opened in 2024 and changed this completely. Ride M11 from Istanbul Airport to Kağıthane, transfer to M2 toward Taksim. Fifty to sixty minutes, about ₺35. The Havaist airport bus is roughly ₺180 for the same trip and gets stuck in traffic. On arrival day with luggage a private transfer (~$40 for a van for four) is worth it; use the metro the rest of the time.

Istanbulkart — buy this first, before anything else

One contactless card covers metro, tram, bus, ferry, funicular and Marmaray — the rail tunnel that runs under the Bosphorus between Europe and Asia. Card costs ₺165, then you load balance separately. A single ride is ₺42, and transfers inside a two-hour window are automatically discounted. Buy it from the yellow or blue Biletmatik machines in any metro, tram or ferry station. One card per person.

The three lines that matter

  • T1 tram (Bağcılar–Kabataş) — the tourist artery. It runs directly past Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, the Grand Bazaar and the Spice Bazaar. You'll use this every day in the Old City.
  • F1 funicular (Taksim–Kabataş) + T1 tram — the single most common route in the city. Learn this connection on day one and everything else opens up.
  • Metro M1–M11 — eleven lines, fastest way to cover distance. The network is modern, clean and cheap.

Ferries — the best money you'll spend

Your Istanbulkart works on every city ferry. Around ₺49 for a crossing to the Asian side. Twenty minutes, tea service on deck, and you're travelling between two continents on a commuter boat. Take the Kadıköy or Üsküdar ferry at least twice, and once after dark.

Taxis

BiTaksi or Uber only — in Istanbul, Uber dispatches licensed yellow cabs, so you get the app protection with a legitimate taxi. Never hail one off the street; meter games are the most common hustle in the city.

Cappadocia

Fly IST → Kayseri (ASR) or Nevşehir (NAV), ninety minutes, $40–70 each on Turkish or Pegasus. Hotels arrange transfers (about an hour from Kayseri). On the ground the region is spread out and there's no real public transport — book activities with pickup, or hire a driver for a day. A rental car works if someone's willing to drive.

Istanbul By Neighborhood

Sultanahmet
The Old City · daytime only

Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque, the Basilica Cistern, Topkapı Palace and the Grand Bazaar are all within a fifteen-minute walk of each other. This is where you spend a full day — and where you do not stay or eat dinner. It empties out at night and the restaurants are tourist-priced.

Karaköy & Galata
Your base · cocktails and rooftops

Waterfront warehouses converted into cocktail bars, third-wave coffee, galleries, and the 1580 hammam. Galata Tower is up the hill. This is the most walkable good-night-out neighborhood in the city and it's where you should be sleeping.

Beyoğlu & Taksim
The engine room · bars, clubs, live music

İstiklal Caddesi runs the spine of it, with dozens of bars, hidden passages and clubs threaded off both sides. Underground techno and house rooms sit alongside old meyhanes. Loud, dense, open very late.

Kadıköy
Asian side · where Istanbul actually drinks

A twenty-minute ferry from Karaköy. Barlar Sokağı — literally Bars Street — plus Kadife Sokak around it. Cheaper drinks, younger crowd, no dress code, essentially no tourists. Also the best food market in the city.

Balat & Fener
Golden Horn · the half-day nobody plans

Painted Ottoman row houses climbing steep cobbled hills — the old Greek and Jewish quarters, still largely undiscovered. The Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople, spiritual seat for 300 million Orthodox Christians, sits quietly here. So does the Iron Church, built entirely of prefabricated cast iron shipped from Vienna in 1898, and St. Mary of the Mongols, the only Byzantine church in the city never converted into a mosque. Antique shops sell Ottoman ceramics and old Turkish records at fair prices. Budget four hours.

What To Actually See

The unmissable

Hagia SophiaSultanahmet

Built in 537. Cathedral, then mosque, then museum, now mosque again. Nothing else on earth has that résumé. Book the timed entry online — the walk-up line is punishing. Go at opening.

Basilica CisternSultanahmet

An underground Roman water chamber held up by 336 columns. Two of them rest on carved Medusa heads nobody can fully explain — one sideways, one upside down.

Topkapı PalaceSultanahmet

Where the Ottoman sultans actually lived for 400 years. The Harem is a separate ticket and it's the better half.

Grand BazaarSultanahmet

4,000 shops under one roof, trading continuously since 1461. Haggling is expected — start at 40% of the asking price and walk if it stalls.

Bosphorus by private boatFrom Karaköy

$200–280 total for the four of you, 2–3 hours. Palaces, waterfront mansions, Europe on one side and Asia on the other. Do this rather than the packed public cruise.

Kılıç Ali Paşa HamamıKaraköy

A working bathhouse from 1580. Steam room, a coarse kese scrub that removes a layer of you, then a foam massage on hot marble. $60–90. Best jet-lag and hangover cure available anywhere.

The ones people skip and shouldn't

Süleymaniye MosqueFatih

Better architecture than the Blue Mosque, a better view over the Golden Horn, and a fraction of the crowd. Mimar Sinan's masterpiece.

Ferry to Kadıköy~₺49

Twenty minutes between continents with tea on deck. Do it once in daylight and once at night with the city lit up.

Galata BridgeEminönü

A hundred men fishing off the rail while you eat a grilled fish sandwich standing up underneath them.

Princes' IslandsBüyükada · ferry day trip

Nine islands 20km offshore, four inhabited. Büyükada is the big one — no cars allowed, wooden mansions, pine forest. A full day, and the right call if anyone wants a slower one.

Food — This Is Half The Trip

Türkiye now holds 17 Michelin-starred restaurants in the 2026 guide, 15 of them one-star, plus 39 Bib Gourmand venues — Michelin's high-quality-for-the-money category. Fatih Tutak holds two stars and is the most serious Turkish fine dining in the world. Newly starred for 2026: Şehzade Erzincanlı and Rumelihisarı İskele, both promoted up from Bib Gourmand.

The one big dinner

MiklaPera · $70–95

Rooftop, Turkish-Scandinavian, ingredients pulled from across Anatolia. Named after the old Viking word for Istanbul. Book weeks ahead.

NeolokalKaraköy · $70–95

Michelin Star and Green Star. Modern technique on traditional Anatolian cooking, in the Salt Galata building with a view over the Golden Horn.

TURK Fatih TutakTwo stars · $150+

If the group wants to go all the way. Intimate, choreographed, and the highest expression of Turkish cooking anywhere.

Ocakbaşı — grilling over open coals

Zübeyir OcakbaşıBeyoğlu · ~$20 a head

You sit around an open coal pit and the cook works in front of you. Lamb ribs, adana, liver skewers. Better than most steakhouses at a tenth of the price. Do this at least twice.

Meyhane — the long night

Asmalı CavitAsmalımescit, Beyoğlu

Rakı, endless cold and hot meze, live saz music, and a table that gets progressively louder over four hours. This is the most Turkish night available and it is not optional.

The Asian side

Çiya SofrasıKadıköy

Regional Anatolian dishes you won't find anywhere on the European side. The chef is a food anthropologist as much as a cook. Cheap, packed, essential.

Everyday and excellent

Karaköy LokantasıKaraköy

Classic Turkish, tiled interior, mid-priced, never wrong. Good first-night landing spot.

TatbakBib Gourmand 2026

Recently promoted by Michelin for pide, kebab and lahmacun. Cheap and excellent.

Yanyalı Fehmi LokantasıKadıköy · Bib Gourmand

An institution. Classic lokanta home cooking, on the new Michelin value list.

Turkish breakfast — clear a morning for it

A proper serpme kahvaltı lands as thirty to forty small plates: five cheeses, olives, honey with clotted cream, eggs cooked in butter with sucuk, tomatoes, jams, fresh bread, and endless tea. It runs $12–20 a head and takes two hours. Do it on your last Istanbul morning.

Street level

Balık ekmek — grilled fish sandwich at Eminönü, a few dollars. Simit — sesame bread ring, sold everywhere, under a dollar. Midye dolma — stuffed mussels with lemon, sold from carts at night, priced per piece. Kokoreç — if anyone's feeling brave. Turkish coffee, then flip the cup and get someone to read your grounds.

Nightlife

How the week should run

Friday (arrival) — easy. Dinner in Karaköy, cocktails on the Soho House roof, in bed by one. You're banking sleep.

Saturday — the big one. Dinner at Mikla or Neolokal, then the Ortaköy waterfront. Saturday is peak and the rooms are full.

Sunday — Kadıköy. Ferry across, eat at Çiya, drink on Barlar Sokağı with locals, ferry back at 1am.

Wednesday (back from Cappadocia) — rooftops. Istanbul does not treat midweek as a dead night.

Thursday — last night. Ocakbaşı feast then a Karaköy crawl with no plan and no alarm.

One rule that matters more than the rest. Never follow a stranger to a bar. The Taksim scam runs on exactly your profile — a friendly, well-dressed English speaker offers to show you a great place, drinks appear, women join the table, and the bill lands at several thousand dollars with the door blocked. Pick your own venues from this list. If you're inside somewhere and the room feels wrong, leave before you order.

Cappadocia

The balloon

Sunrise launch, hundreds of balloons in the air at once over eroded volcanic spires. Book direct with Butterfly Balloons, Royal Balloon or Voyager — $180–250 each, and skip the resellers. Balloons fly roughly 90% of May mornings, among the highest fly rates of the year. Pickup is 4:30am. Schedule it your first Cappadocia morning so the second stays a weather backup.

Everything else

Göreme Open-Air MuseumUNESCO

A cluster of rock-cut Byzantine churches with frescoes still on the walls, painted by early Christian communities hiding in the rock.

Derinkuyu underground cityOr Kaymaklı

Eight levels carved down into volcanic rock. Stables, chapels, wineries and ventilation shafts. It sheltered 20,000 people from invading armies.

ATVs at golden hour~$45 each

Love Valley and Rose Valley on quad bikes as the light goes. In May the valleys are full of wildflowers.

Rose Valley hikeFree

Also the best highland vantage point for watching the balloons from the ground if yours gets weathered out.

Uçhisar Castle & Pigeon ValleyHalf day

A rock fortress with the widest view in the region, then walk down through Pigeon Valley to Göreme.

Avanos potteryOn the Kızılırmak

A town that's been making red-clay pottery on foot-powered wheels since the Hittites. You can throw a pot badly and take it home.

Ortahisar Castle & Imagination ValleyFiller options

Good half-day fallbacks if the balloon backup morning goes unused.

Money & Practicalities

VisaNone. US citizens are visa-free for 90 days. Passport valid six months past 28 May 2027 with a blank page. Check all four now, not in April.
CurrencyLira at roughly ₺47.7 to the dollar. Withdraw from bank ATMs — Ziraat, İş Bankası, Garanti — not the standalone machines in tourist zones.
CardsAccepted almost everywhere in Istanbul. Carry cash in Cappadocia. Always decline "pay in USD" at terminals and ATMs — dynamic currency conversion is a 5–10% skim.
PhoneeSIM via Airalo or Holafly, $10–20 for the trip. Don't buy a local physical SIM — Turkey registers device IMEIs.
AlcoholHeavily taxed. $6–9 a beer, $10–14 a cocktail. Duty-free on arrival at IST is genuinely worth using for hotel drinks.
Tipping5–10% at restaurants, round up taxis, $5–10 for hammam attendants.
MosquesLong pants, shoes off, shoulders covered. Avoid Friday midday prayers. Free to enter.
SafetyLevel 2 — same tier as France and Italy. The Level 4 zone is the southeast near the Syria border, roughly 900 miles from anything here. Register with STEP before you go.
