The Two Landmines

Japan — Golden Week, 29 April to 5 May 2027

Four national holidays plus a bridge day on Friday 30 April gives most Japanese workers seven straight days off. The entire country travels at once. Trains, buses and flights sell out, and the Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka corridor is the worst-hit route in the country. Outbound rush builds 28–30 April, return wave hits 4–5 May, most people back at work on the 6th.

Hotel rates spike and availability collapses. Do not book Japan before 7 May. This is the single most avoidable mistake on the calendar.

Turkey — Kurban Bayramı, 15 to 19 May 2027

Eid al-Adha, the most important holiday in the Muslim calendar. Arife (the eve) falls Saturday 15 May, the first day is Sunday 16 May, and it runs through the evening of Wednesday 19 May.

What actually closes: the Grand Bazaar and the Spice Bazaar shut entirely. Banks and post offices close. ATMs frequently run dry toward the end of the holiday. Domestic roads and flights are chaos on the first and last days as the whole country either goes home or heads for the coast.

What stays open: most museums and historical sites, plus shops and supermarkets. So Hagia Sophia is fine — the bazaars are not. Book either 1–13 May or 20–31 May.

Minor, not disqualifying: Korea has Children's Day on 5 May and Buddha's Birthday on Thursday 13 May 2027. Both are single days. Palaces and temples get busy; nothing shuts down. Plan around them if it's easy, ignore them if it isn't.

The pick

Turkey. 20–31 May 2027. Nine days, around $3,290 a head.

It won the balanced scorecard, May is its strongest season, the Bayram window closes cleanly on the 19th, and it's still the shortest trip in days off work — nine days door to door, ten hours in the air, a seven-hour shift you'll shake off in a day. Korea stays the value play at $2,945 if the group wants to spend less and drink more. Japan is one point behind on the scorecard and wins if food is the deciding vote.
