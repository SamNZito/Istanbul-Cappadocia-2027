Two things that are not negotiable about October

Thailand cannot start before October 20. Early October is 180mm of rain in Bangkok and up to 300mm on the Andaman coast. After the 20th it flips — Phuket and Krabi become reliable, Chiang Mai leaves the rainy season, Bangkok storms ease. If the group can't align on late-October dates, Thailand is off and this is a Turkey trip.

The 7-day Thailand option is priced but I don't recommend it. Twenty-plus hours each way for five nights on the ground. It's the cheapest number on the sheet and the worst value on it.

Hotels vs Airbnb — The Short Answer

Book hotels. Two reasons, and the second one is the surprising one.

1 · It's a legal mess in both countries. Turkey's Law No. 7464 — the "100-day law" — has been fully enforced since January 2026. Any rental under 100 days requires a permit that needs unanimous approval from every owner in the building. Most listings don't have it, and inspections and license revocations are active. In Thailand, sub-30-day condo rentals are illegal without a hotel license under the Hotel Act; penalties run to a year's imprisonment plus 20,000 THB and 10,000/day, and there was a raid on 191 rooms in Pratunam. Enforcement targets hosts — but guests are who ends up on the sidewalk at 11pm.

2 · The savings barely exist here. A 2-bedroom Istanbul apartment runs $180–250/night against $440 for two good hotel rooms. That's ~$50 per person per night — and you're trading away breakfast, housekeeping, a front desk that solves problems at 2am, better locations, and legal certainty. The Airbnb arbitrage that works at home mostly evaporates when hotel rooms cost $110–220.

The middle option: a licensed serviced apartment or aparthotel. Apartment layout, separate bedrooms, a kitchen and shared living room to pre-game in, plus a real front desk and an actual license. Both Istanbul and Bangkok have good ones, and pricing sits between the two columns. If the group wants one shared space, this is the version to book — not a private listing.
