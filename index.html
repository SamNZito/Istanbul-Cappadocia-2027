Nightlife

Local rhythm: clubs don't get going until after midnight and covers usually start at 11pm. Eat late, arrive later. Bars close 1–2am, clubs run to 4–5am. The 10pm alcohol ban is retail only — shops and supermarkets. Licensed venues serve well past midnight, so your nights are unaffected.

Sortie / ReinaOrtaköy waterfront

Bottle service on the Bosphorus under the bridge. Your Saturday. Dress code enforced on men.

KastelBeyoğlu

Rooftop club, local and international DJs, Bosphorus views. Best of both formats.

RXTaksim · techno

Funktion-One rig, big LED wall, proper floor. The serious electronic room.

360 Istanbul · 16 Roof · Seven HillsRooftops

360 turns into a club as the night goes. 16 Roof has the best sunset drink in the city. Seven Hills puts the Blue Mosque, Hagia Sophia and the Bosphorus in one sightline.

Barlar SokağıKadıköy · Asian side

An entire street of bars. Cheapest drinks, best crowd, zero tourists. Don't skip it.

Alexandra · Nardis JazzGalata

Serious cocktails in a small room, and live jazz for the slower night.

The one rule that matters most. Never follow a stranger to a bar. The Taksim scam runs specifically on groups of foreign men — a friendly English speaker offers to show you a great place, drinks appear, women join the table, and the bill lands at several thousand dollars with the door blocked. Pick your own venues from this list. If a room feels wrong, leave before you order. Emergency number in Türkiye: 112.

Getting Around

  • From the airport: the M11 metro opened in 2024 — ride to Kağıthane, transfer to M2 for Taksim. 50–60 min, about ₺35. The Havaist bus is ₺180 and sits in traffic. On arrival day with bags, a private van (~$40 for four) is worth it.
  • Istanbulkart: buy one each on day one from any Biletmatik machine. ₺165 for the card, then load balance. ₺42 a ride with discounted transfers inside two hours. Covers metro, tram, bus, ferry, funicular and Marmaray.
  • T1 tram runs past Hagia Sophia, the Blue Mosque and both bazaars. Daily use.
  • Funiculars beat hills. The Tünel — second-oldest underground railway in the world, running since 1875 — hauls you from Galata Bridge up to İstiklal.
  • Ferries, ~₺49. Twenty minutes between continents. Do it once in daylight, once at night.
  • Taxis: BiTaksi or Uber only. Uber dispatches licensed yellow cabs here. Never hail off the street.
  • Traffic: Istanbul has among the worst road congestion of any major city — drivers lose 53% extra time. Stay on rails and boats.

Entry & Laws

No visa. But check your passport.

  • US citizens are visa-free for 90 days. No e-visa, no fee. This changed in 2024 — older guides are wrong.
  • Passport valid six months past entry — for 20 May 2027, that means through 20 November 2027. Plus one blank page.
  • No vaccination requirements. Register with STEP before you fly. Get travel insurance, ~$50–90.

Do not insult the President, Atatürk, or the flag

Actively prosecuted, including for social media posts by foreigners. Insulting Atatürk's memory or the flag carries up to five years under Law 5816. No political jokes, no political posts while you're in the country. His portrait is everywhere and he's genuinely revered — costs nothing to respect.

Do not take home anything old — including a rock

Exporting antiquities carries 5 to 12 years in prison. Tourists have been arrested for pocketing an interesting stone or a pottery shard at a ruin. Take nothing off the ground. If you buy something that might read as antique, use an authorized dealer and keep the museum certificate plus receipt in your carry-on.

Drugs — zero tolerance

Severe penalties for any quantity, cannabis included. No tourist leniency. Don't carry anything, don't accept anything.
