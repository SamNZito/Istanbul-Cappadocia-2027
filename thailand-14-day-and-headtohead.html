Thailand at Fourteen

The full 14-day Thailand build at the same fidelity as the Turkey plan, plus a direct side-by-side so you can see exactly what each extra day buys you. Nothing is decided here.

Prepared 8 August 2026 · Companion to Where the Dollar Actually Wins · 4 travelers, US East Coast

The Thing That Probably Decides This

Turkey and Thailand have non-overlapping best seasons. Turkey peaks September–October and April–June. Thailand's Andaman coast peaks December–March. This isn't a tiebreaker — it may be the entire decision.

Reading the calendar: Thailand's two coasts run opposite monsoons. The Andaman side (Phuket, Krabi, Phi Phi) is wettest May–October, peaking in September. The Gulf side (Samui, Phangan, Koh Tao) is wettest October–December, peaking in November. October is the worst month to plan a Thai beach trip — both coasts are bad simultaneously. Turkey is hot and overpriced in July–August and cools off after late October.

Practical read: If you want to go in the next few months — Turkey. If you're willing to wait for winter and take 14 days — Thailand. If you land on September specifically, both work, but Thailand has to be a Gulf-coast trip (variant B below), not the Phuket/Krabi one everyone pictures.

The number that matters most: cost per usable day. Thailand runs about $342 per day on the ground versus Turkey's $526 — roughly 35% cheaper once you're actually there. Thailand is unambiguously the better value.

But it costs three extra workdays per person and hands you a 12-hour time shift. So the honest framing is: Thailand wins on money, Turkey wins on time. Which one you're shorter on is the answer.

Plan C — Thailand Andaman · 14 Days Door to Door

Bangkok (3 nights) → Chiang Mai (3 nights) → Krabi / Railay (4 nights). Ten nights on the ground, two domestic flights, three bases. Built for December–March. Two rooms shared between four.

Day 0–1 · Departure
JFK/EWR → Bangkok

One stop, 20–22 hours total. Best routings go through Doha (Qatar), Istanbul (Turkish), Tokyo (ANA/JAL), or Taipei (EVA). Depart Friday evening, land Sunday around 1am. You lose a day and a half — this is the tax you're paying for the exchange rate.

Book the aisle seats and take the connection over the shortest total time. A 3-hour layover where you can walk around beats a 55-minute sprint every time on this route.

Day 2 · Bangkok
Land, temples, riverside

Arrive early morning. Take the metered taxi or a Grab — a legitimate metered ride from Suvarnabhumi to central Bangkok is 250–400 THB ($8–12). Anyone quoting a 500–1,000 THB flat fare is running the standard airport hustle.

Stay: Anantara Riverside (~$190/room) or Chatrium Riverside (~$140/room) for value; Mandarin Oriental, Peninsula, or Capella at $350–700 if you want the icon. Riverside is the right call — the hotel boats are free and beat Bangkok traffic entirely.

Day: Grand Palace and Wat Pho at opening, Wat Arun across the river at sunset. Night: easy. You're 12 hours off. Rooftop at Sky Bar or Vertigo, then sleep.

Day 3 · Bangkok
Markets, Muay Thai, Sukhumvit

Day: Chatuchak Weekend Market if it's Sat/Sun (15,000 stalls), otherwise Talad Noi and Chinatown for a street food crawl. Yaowarat Road after dark is one of the best eating streets on earth.

Evening: Muay Thai at Rajadamnern Stadium. Ringside from 2,500 THB (~$75), club class 1,900 THB, VIP lounge with drinks 4,500 THB. Book the real stadium, not a tourist show.

Night: Thonglor and Ekkamai for the good bars, or Sukhumvit Soi 11 for the loud version.

Day 4 · Bangkok
Ayutthaya day trip

Ancient capital, an hour north — sprawling temple ruins, ~$50 each with a driver for four. Back by late afternoon. Massage ($10–15 for an hour, and you'll get several this trip), then a proper dinner. Bo.lan or Nahm for high-end Thai, or Jay Fai if you can get in.

Day 5 · Chiang Mai
Fly north

Morning flight BKK → CNX, ~75 minutes, $30–50 on Thai Vietjet, Nok Air, or Thai Smile. Chiang Mai is 40–60% cheaper than the islands and it's where the trip gets good value.

Stay: Nimman district for nightlife and cafés, or Old City for temples. Good 5-star runs ~$110/room. Night: Nimman bar street, night bazaar.

Day 6 · Chiang Mai
Elephant sanctuary, jungle

Full-day ethical elephant sanctuary — no riding, no bullhooks. Elephant Nature Park is the reference standard. ~$70 each including transport and lunch. Book weeks ahead; the legitimate ones sell out.

Afternoon/evening: Doi Suthep temple at sunset over the valley, then back down for dinner.

Day 7 · Chiang Mai
Ziplines, waterfalls, Sunday walking street

Jungle zipline course (~$60) or a Mae Taeng day with waterfalls and bamboo rafting. Sticky Waterfalls if you'd rather do something free and genuinely strange — the limestone grips your feet and you climb straight up a waterfall.

Night: Last Chiang Mai night. Cheap, easy, no dress code anywhere.

Day 8 · Krabi
Fly south to the Andaman

CNX → KBV, usually connecting through Bangkok, ~$60–90. Note: the direct Chiang Mai–Krabi flight is seasonal and often unavailable May–October.

Stay: Railay Beach for the dramatic version — accessible only by longtail boat, ringed by limestone cliffs, no roads. Ao Nang for more restaurants and bars with easier logistics. Budget ~$200/room for a good resort.

Day 9 · Krabi
Private longtail charter — four islands

Charter the whole boat, don't buy seats on a shared tour. 4,500–6,500 THB for a full day (~$135–195 for the boat), so $35–50 each. National park fees run 200–400 THB extra per person. Chicken Island, Tup Island, Poda, and the sandbar you can walk between islands on at low tide. Your own boat means you set the schedule and skip the crowds.

Day 10 · Krabi
Phi Phi day, or rock climbing

Option A: Speedboat to Koh Phi Phi and Maya Bay, ~$60 each. Go early; by 11am it's a parking lot of boats.

Option B: Rock climbing at Railay — world-famous limestone, half-day beginner course ~$35 with a guide and all gear. Even for people who've never climbed.

Night: Ao Nang beach bars, fire shows, buckets. Not sophisticated. Extremely fun.

Day 11 · Krabi
Nothing scheduled

Deliberately empty. Ten days in, four guys need one day where the plan is a beach chair, a Chang, and a massage. If the group wants more, the Hong Islands lagoon and Emerald Pool are both close.

Day 12–13 · Return
KBV → BKK → JFK

Morning flight to Bangkok (~$45), long layover, evening departure west. You gain the hours back — land on the East Coast the same calendar day. Leave a 4-hour minimum at BKK; it's a big airport and immigration lines are real.

Plan C-Lean — the cheaper Thailand

Same 14-day route, 3-star boutiques and guesthouses instead of resorts, shoulder-season airfare, street food as the default, and half the paid activities. Roughly $2,000–2,200 per person all-in. Thailand is the only destination on either list where you can run a two-week trip at that number and still have it be a good trip — this is what the exchange rate actually buys you.

Variant B — The Gulf Coast Trip

If you go July–September, you cannot do the Andaman route. Flip to the Gulf side, which is a different — and for four guys, arguably better — trip.

Bangkok 3N → Koh Tao 3N → Koh Phangan 3N → Koh Samui 1N.

  • Koh Tao — the cheapest place in the world to get PADI Open Water certified. Roughly $300–350 for the full 3–4 day course including accommodation at most dive shops. Four guys leaving Thailand as certified divers is a genuinely good trip outcome.
  • Koh Phangan — the Full Moon Party at Haad Rin. Monthly on the actual full moon, 9pm to sunrise, 200 THB (~$6) cash at the gate, no online tickets. Fourteen parties in 2026 including a New Year's Eve edition on 31 December. Check the exact date before booking flights — it shifts with the lunar calendar and occasionally moves 1–2 days around national holidays.
  • Koh Samui — has the airport, so it's your exit. One night, decompress, fly out.

Costs land slightly below Plan C — call it $2,900–3,400 per person — because Koh Tao and Koh Phangan are cheaper than Krabi and you drop a domestic flight.

Thailand Logistics

Visa — this changed and it matters

US citizens get a 30-day visa exemption on arrival — reduced from 60 days in a recent rule change. A 14-day trip is comfortably inside that, but confirm the current rule before you book, and have proof of onward travel ready. Passport valid 6+ months.

Scams — the 2026 versions

  • Transport overcharging. The big one. Suvarnabhumi to central Bangkok is 250–400 THB metered. Use Grab (Southeast Asia's Uber) and skip the negotiation entirely.
  • Rental deposit hostage. Jet ski, motorbike, and car rental shops that keep your passport and then invent damage. Never hand over your passport as a deposit. Photograph every scratch before you take the keys. Jet ski scams in Phuket and Pattaya are notorious.
  • "The Grand Palace is closed today." It isn't. A helpful stranger redirects you to a tuk-tuk driver who takes you to a gem or tailor shop instead. Walk away.
  • Tourist Police: dial 1155. 24/7, English-speaking. Free. Use it if anything goes sideways.

Everything else

  • Money: Baht at ~33/USD. ATMs charge a flat 220 THB foreign fee, so withdraw the maximum each time. Decline dynamic currency conversion. Cash is king outside hotels.
  • Phone: eSIM via Airalo, or grab an AIS/TrueMove tourist SIM at the airport for ~$10 — both fine here, Thailand doesn't have Turkey's IMEI registration issue.
  • Apps: Grab (rides + food), 12Go (trains, ferries, buses), Google Translate with Thai downloaded offline.
  • Health: No vaccines required. Drink bottled water. Bangkok's private hospitals (Bumrungrad, Samitivej) are excellent and cheap — a doctor visit runs $30–50. Get travel insurance that covers diving if you're doing Koh Tao; standard policies often exclude it.
  • Temple etiquette: Shoulders and knees covered, shoes off, never point your feet at a Buddha image. The Grand Palace enforces this at the gate.
  • Drugs: Cannabis was decriminalized and then re-restricted; the rules are in flux and enforcement is inconsistent. Everything else carries penalties that are severe and not negotiable. Don't.

How I'd Decide

  1. When can all four of you actually get time off? If it's this fall, the season calendar picks Turkey for you. If it's after Thanksgiving, it picks Thailand.
  2. Is anyone tight on PTO? Thailand costs three more workdays per person. If one guy can't swing it, you're down to three travelers or you're going to Turkey.
  3. What does the group actually want most? If the honest answer is beaches and nightlife, Thailand wins and it isn't close. If it's food, history, and one trip where you're not exhausted, Turkey.
  4. Budget is nearly a wash. $3,140–3,690 versus $3,450–3,920. Do not let a $300 difference decide a trip this size — decide on time and season, then spend accordingly.
