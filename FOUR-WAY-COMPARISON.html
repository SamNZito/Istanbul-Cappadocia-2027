Turkey is the most expensive of the four on the ground

That reverses the premise you opened with. Istanbul hotel prices have climbed hard — Turkey has high domestic inflation, so the lira collapse doesn't pass through to you one-for-one. And alcohol is heavily taxed: $6–9 a beer, $10–14 a cocktail, versus $2–4 a beer in Bangkok and Seoul.

For a group whose plan includes drinking every night, that gap compounds fast. Turkey still wins on total trip cost for the fewest days off work — it's the only option here that fits in nine days. It just isn't the cheap one anymore.

What You'd Actually Do

Japan — everything works, nothing disappoints

Nights: Golden Gai, six alleys of 200 bars each seating six people. Omoide Yokocho for smoke-filled yakitori counters. Private karaoke rooms all night. Standing bars in Osaka's Temma district. Dotonbori neon at midnight.

Days: Fushimi Inari at 6am with 10,000 gates empty. Shibuya Sky over the crossing. teamLab. Hakone onsen with Fuji views, or Nikko for real autumn color in late October. Japanese baseball, which is unhinged. One kaiseki dinner.

South Korea — best nights, cheapest total

Nights: Three distinct zones — Hongdae (young, indie, ~₩15,000 entry), Itaewon (international, English-friendly), Gangnam (luxury, tables from ₩300,000). Korean BBQ and soju for hours. Noraebang. Chimaek — fried chicken and beer delivered to you on the grass by the Han River.

Days: DMZ tour, standing a hundred yards from North Korea. Gwangjang Market food stalls. Gyeongbokgung Palace (free entry in a hanbok). Korean baseball. And jjimjilbang — a 24-hour bathhouse where you sweat out the night and sleep on a heated floor for $10. Best hangover infrastructure on earth.

Turkey — best stories, fewest days off

Nights: Rooftops over the Bosphorus. Meyhane with rakı, endless meze, and live saz. Ocakbaşı grills where you sit around an open coal pit for $20. Karaköy cocktail bars, Kadıköy for locals, Ortaköy waterfront clubs (dress code enforced).

Days: Sunrise balloon over Cappadocia — October has the lowest cancellation rate of the year at 7–17%. A hammam built in 1580. Private Bosphorus boat for $65 a head. Underground cities eight levels deep. A dollar ferry between two continents.

Thailand — everything costs nothing

Nights: Ringside Muay Thai at Rajadamnern, $75. Yaowarat Road in Chinatown at midnight. Rooftops, then Thonglor. Beer for $2.

Days: Elephants in Chiang Mai. The Monk's Trail to a temple built into a waterfall. Monk Chat, where young monks ask you as many questions as you ask them. Private longtail charter for $45 each. Rock climbing at Railay. Hour-long massages for $10.

The Fifth Option Nobody Priced

Japan + South Korea — $3,955, 13 days

Tokyo to Seoul is 2h39m for $117–213. Fly into Tokyo, out of Seoul, open-jaw. Tokyo 4N, Kyoto 3N, Seoul 4N. Both countries are at October peak, both require no visa, and both have zero friction. $360 per night on the ground — cheaper per day than Turkey, for two countries instead of one.

It's the most expensive line on this page and the best trip on this page. If the group can do 13 days, this is what I'd book.

My actual recommendation

South Korea, 10 days, $2,945. It is the cheapest total on the board, has the best nightlife of the four, sits at peak season in October, needs no visa, requires zero problem-solving, and costs three fewer days off work than Thailand.

You opened this by asking for a country where the dollar makes you rich and the nights are good. Of everything still standing, Korea is the one that delivers both — and it's the only option that's simultaneously the cheapest and the best at the thing you care most about. If the group will stretch to 13 days and $4k, add Tokyo and Kyoto to the front of it and you have the trip of the decade.
